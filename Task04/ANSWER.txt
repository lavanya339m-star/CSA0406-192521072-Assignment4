4.2 Simulation
A meaningful simulation should model arrival times, service order, request completion times and waiting times for 100, 1,000 and 10,000 requests under light/medium/heavy arrival rates. The included C program demonstrates the fixed queue; do not claim its output is a 10,000-request timing benchmark. For a full simulation, generate deterministic seeded arrivals and report mean and maximum waiting time separately.

4.3 Fairness
SSTF may starve a distant request if nearer requests keep arriving. FCFS is fair by arrival order but may have high movement. SCAN sweeps in both directions; C-SCAN gives more uniform directional waiting because requests are serviced in one direction before a wrap.

4.4 SSD
SSDs have no mechanical seek arm, so cylinder movement is not a useful optimisation target. Linux may use none for low-overhead NVMe paths or mq-deadline where latency/fairness is desired. Check: cat /sys/block/<disk>/queue/scheduler (replace <disk> with actual device from lsblk).
LOOK/C-LOOK stop at last pending request rather than disk boundary; this reduces unnecessary boundary travel. Sorting requests costs O(n log n); a linear scan to find extrema costs O(n).
